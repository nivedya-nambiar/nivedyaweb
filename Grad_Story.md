# Grad Story

Over the last few years, people reached out to me on LinkedIn asking for different kinds of grad school advice ranging from purely logistical (so what documents exactly did you need) to almost career counseling (which university do I pick?) to borderline existential (but *why* did you do *that*, and what are *your* plans). This blogpost is an attempt to condense all that information to a page approximately clustering different kinds of questions prospective students might have before I forget it👵. In no way exhaustive, and filled with overlaps, this summary is a personal recounting of experience which I suggest you read with a generous seasoning of salt. All this to say, I hope you judge your own circumstances as you do mine when you take any decision after reading this. 🙂‍↕️.

## A little about me
I'm Nivedya and I'm from a place called Keralam 🌴 in India. I did my Bachelor's (BTech) in Electrical Engineering from IIT Bombay where I developed an interest in neuroscience & psychology and decided to do some courses and projects that 'connected' neuroscience with my EE program - this meant any course that had the word neuroscience/psychology in it, doing my bachelor thesis project in neuromorphic engineering, and watching neuroscience lectures to build more context in neuroscience (like [The Human Brain by Nancy Kanwisher](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-13-the-human-brain-spring-2019/)). After my degree, I briefly worked as a research assistant at a Cognitive Neuroscience Lab and worked on my grad school application on the side. I attended [Neuromatch Academy's online computational neuroscience summer school](https://compneuro.neuromatch.io/tutorials/intro.html). I eventually had the good fortune of being accepted into [EPFL's Neuro-X Master program](https://www.epfl.ch/education/master/programs/neuro-x/). Thanks to the program's flexibility, I completed courses ranging from neurobiology to computational neuroscience to neuroAI and projects in fMRI/EEG where I got hands-on experience with data analysis and even acquisition.

## Grad Application
Grad applications are daunting for sure with all of its uncertainty - deciding whether it is a good time to go to graduate school, finding a university/program that meets your criteria, tracking application deadlines and fulfilling the requirements, managing your own expectations of the outcomes. What helped for me was to phase out my application process, once I was fairly sure I wanted to shoot my shot applying to master/PhD programs in neuroscience after completing my Electrical Engineering bachelor's degree. (P.S. Most of the steps I describe below apply to a Master's application, so not readily extended to a PhD application process.)

**Phase I** was simply searching for potential programs and listing them in a spreadsheet. Here's a [spreadsheet template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1lYtDGQlR41Ki9k3MYVPaY8o-VuAmLN7sX3_BL5CXAcQ/edit?usp=sharing) I developed to do a brain dump of all the programs that were even remotely interesting. I included important deadlines and mapped out the requirements and estimated expenses, filtering out options I could not meet the eligibility for. I slowly also developed a personal ranking between the options based on interest, university, location etc. to understand how to approach multiple applications with the limited time/effort reserve. It is also helpful to imagine a division of your list into three categories - (i) * dream * programs with the lowest chance of acceptance (for instance it could be a great university with competitive admission), (ii) achievable-with-effort programs, (iii) safe bets (e.g. if you are someone who is switching fields like I did, this could be a graduate program continuing your major that allows you to pursue your intended field as a minor). Needless to say, I kept appending to this list as I came across more interesting options during my application journey. 

This phase is also a good time to consider whether there are extra certifications/projects that can help you upskill and thereby increase your chances at admission. For instance [Neuromatch Academy's courses](https://neuromatch.io/courses/) that provide online certification are well-recognized by universities for neuroscience programs. If there are programs that require additional stuff like language certification, prepare for these accordingly. It could also help to map out scholarship opportunities and their specific eligibility requirements and deadlines.

**Phase 2** was actually getting down to * do * the applications once I mapped out the main requirements - usually a statement of purpose (SoP), CV and letters of recommendation. Reach out to your referees ahead of time to request for *strong* reference letters, typically from supervisors of your past projects who will be able to vouch for your work, knowledge & soft skills. 

While the exact requirements vary for each program, it helps to have a 'master' SoP (excuse the pun) and CV which you tailor to each application's specific requirements such as word/page limits. Word of advice from my own seniors that worked well - do not look at other people's statements to start writing your own but only after your get your first draft ready. Your first draft is by no means perfect but rather meant to serve as a brain dump (answering the why did you choose the program, what experiences make you a good candidate, what do you intend to do with this program) and defining the overall flow of the document. Get your SoP reviewed with seniors you know and incorporate the feedbacks over multiple iterations.

**Phase 3** was completing the applications by fine-tuning SoPs/CVs to individual program requirements, and completing other steps like interviews etc.

...and waiting for the results, occasionally checking for other related programs that I could apply to.

## Master's In Neuro-X at EPFL
Head to [the Neuro-X Master website](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/master-program-in-neuro-x/) for the latest course plan, program details and admission requirements. I'll be describing below the requirements I went through for my application to the September 2024 intake.

### Application Logistics
The application was done through an [online portal](https://www.epfl.ch/education/admission/admission-2/master-admission-criteria-application/online-application/) where I submitted my CV, a 1000 word essay describing why I wanted to join the program (background & objectives of study), and 3 letters of recommendation from professors who supervised my past projects (they receive an email where they are required to fill a recommendation form). The application did not require GRE as a mandatory requirement but you may have to produce an English certification (TOEFL/IELTS etc) if you aren't able to produce proof for your previous degree being taught in English. The application had 2 deadlines - Dec 15 2023 and Mar 31 2023- both for the same intake, and I submitted to the Dec 15 one to leave ample time for visa processing after receiving the outcome. The application required a fee of 150 CHF, as I had a foreign Bachelor degree. I received the outcome of acceptance via email towards the end of March 2024, after which I proceeded to complete the admin steps described in this email and commence [visa application](#visa-application-for-indian-nationals) & [house-hunting](#housing).

### Scholarships
The EPFL master program application automatically includes an application for the Excellence Fellowship which is awarded to a limited set of candidates per cycle. Head to [EPFL's scholarships information page](https://www.epfl.ch/education/studies/en/financing-study/grants/) to know more.

Besides this, you can apply to [specific fellowships](https://www.epfl.ch/education/international/en/studying-abroad/scholarships-for-studies-abroad/) to fund your master thesis if you plan to do it abroad. Examples include the Bertarelli Foundation, WiSH and Zeno Karl Shindler. Bertarelli Foundation's fellowship funds your project at a Harvard Medical School affiliated lab and requires you to apply around your first semester.

### Courses & Projects
[The Neuro-X study plan](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/study-plan/) includes:
* core courses - courses offering fundamentals in neurobiology, neuroimaging, neural interface design and machine learning from which you choose a combination that equals at least 31 credits*
* options courses - here you choose a subset equaling 30 credits* from a broader pool covering technology, data science/ML, neuroscience etc, 
* 2 semester projects*- these are semester-long research projects in collaboration with a research lab/hospital/company. Of these, the first [Neuro-X project I](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/semester-project/) is intended to be completed at an EPFL associated research lab and the second is a [Scientific, industry, clinical project](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/industrial-scientific-and-clinical-project/) which can be completed in clinics or company partners. For EPFL research labs, you will typically find postings on the lab websites for open project positions and alternatively you can directly reach out to teaching assistants in courses who are PhD students at the lab you wish to work with. Typically, a semester project would require you to spend 1-2 day(s)/week working on your project as agreed upon with your supervisor. People also choose to combine these projects into a single intensive project, or complete the projects during summer/winter holidays.
* [Social Science and Humanities] (https://www.epfl.ch/education/programme-shs/) - This cluster includes a two-part course typically completed in semesters 1 and 2 respectively. You can choose one cluster from the [available list](https://edu.epfl.ch/studyplan/en/master/humanities-and-social-sciences-program/) based on your interest and language requirements, and apply for it with the regular registration cycle on EPFL's academic portal ISA (most have limited seats offered on a first-come-first-serve basis).
* [Industry internship](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/industry-internship/) - The mandatory internship can be completed in a company or research organization that is not affiliated to any university (e.g. CHUV Lausanne). To find a position, your best bet is the internship portal on ISA and your own applications through networking/cold-mailing/targeted applications. The minimum duration for an industry internship is 2 months but realistically most companies offer 6-month positions. *Heads up for non-EU nationals doing an internship in EU, your employer is required to get you a work authorization and it is likely that many research organizations aren't aware of the procedures. Communicate with the recruiters to verify that they can definitely provide you with such an authorization.*
* [Master's Thesis Project](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/sections/neuro-x/master-project/) - Once your course and internship requirements have been completed you can start your thesis project (barring cases where you have 1-2 courses remaining that you complete alongside the thesis after getting approval from the section). You may also complete the thesis project in industry if your total credits completed in industry projects (across semester projects+internship+thesis) does not exceed 38* - allowing you to combine your internship and thesis to a single extended project if you manage to get the logistics right.

On average completing all these requirements takes **~2.5 years** (unless you speed-run courses and semester projects in just 2 semesters or find a 2 month internship position).
*-_don't forget to cross-check with the requirements of your year_ 

## Visa Application For Indian Nationals
You will be required to get a Type D Schengen Visa followed by registration with your municipality for a residence permit (Type B) which you will need to renew every year. [This reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/Student_Visas/comments/1tz4fci/switzerland_student_visa_guide_2026_requirements/) is a good summary of main requirements as of 2026. 

Your visa application process mainly involves the following steps
- Organizing the required documents for application according to the latest requirements. Check the [EPFL page on visa requirements](https://www.epfl.ch/education/studies/en/epfl-studies/immigration-en/detail-visa-en/).
- Booking an appointment with the Embassy of Switzerland in New Delhi to submit the documents in person.
- Waiting on the approval and submitting your passport to the nearest VFS centre to send it for stamping. The stamped passport is then delivered to your home address.
Here was the timeline I followed - I began my visa application around the beginning of April, the appointment with the Embassy at the end of May, which led to visa approvaYl by mid-June. Later applications can be subject to higher processing delays because of higher volume of applications.

Once you arrive in Switzerland, you need to register with your municipality within 14 days. I know I'm a broken record here, but check the [EPFL page for information on the residence permit](https://www.epfl.ch/education/studies/en/epfl-studies/immigration-en/residence-permit/) for details. If your residence falls within the municipality of Lausanne, you can complete this registration online, after which you will be sent an attestation via (physical) mail while you wait for the actual residence permit card. This attestation allows you to setup a bank account in Switzerland. You will receive also an invitation (again, check your letter box) to go to the biometric center to provide your details which will be used for the card. Depending on your municipality you will also receive letters asking you to pay one-time fees (residence tax), each time you renew your permit 😪.

## Life In Lausanne, Switzerland
Here I've attempted to describe housing, expenses, language and general stuff about adapting to life in Lausanne I wish I knew earlier.

### Housing
Switzerland is notorious for its sky-high rents but as a Master's student you get access to the slightly more accessible student housing options(https://www.epfl.ch/campus/services/housing/en/). [This reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/EPFL/comments/14awtji/a_little_guide_on_housing_in_lausanne/) is probably one of the most comprehensive pieces of advice out there. I suggest applying as early as possible and to as many places as possible, and to beware of scams (which you can avoid by sticking to legit housing options such as FMEL, Les Estudiantines). In case you are unable to find housing on time, try to look for [temporary accommodation](https://www.epfl.ch/campus/services/housing/housing-options/short-term/) while you search for options in Lausanne/wait on your requests to platforms like FMEL. Be careful that you will need to accordingly adjust the address provided for your residence permit.

### Insurance
You are required to get a mandatory [health insurance](https://www.epfl.ch/education/studies/en/epfl-studies/insurance/health-insurance/) for your time here. There is also ECA (insurance for fire protection) which may or may not already be included in your lease, so you will need to ask your housing/landlord if this is covered already.

### Part-time jobs
You are allowed to take up [part-time jobs](https://www.epfl.ch/education/studies/en/financing-study/working/) after ~6 months of receiving the residence permit. Your best bet is teaching assistant positions (for undergraduate courses or master-level courses; some core Neuro-X courses which you already completed), specific positions available on the [jobs portal](https://jx.epfl.ch/myjob), and in some cases research assistant positions with labs.

### Language
Lausanne is in the French-speaking part of Switzerland and most official documentation/mails will be in French. EPFL has free [French courses](https://www.epfl.ch/campus/services/internal-trainings/language-centre/languages/all-modules-in-french/) offered during the semester and also during breaks which are a great way to learn the language to integrate better into life in Lausanne.
